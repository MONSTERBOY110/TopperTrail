How a claim is recorded and checked

Every flag below is a fixed rule with a named basis. No AI model decides anything; the same evidence always produces the same ledger.

What counts as a claim

  • Only sources an institute controls: its registered website, its YouTube channels, and its own Google ads shown in India.
  • A page counts when it ties the topper to the institute: words such as "our student", "congratulations", "alumni" or "associated with", a named course, or the institute's own name beside the topper. Pages that only list the official toppers as news are recorded as named without claiming.
  • Videos on the institute's own channel, its posters and its ads always count, because publishing them is the association.
  • The exam and year must appear near the name, so a topper of one exam is never matched to a namesake from another.

The topper's own words

For each topper, TopperTrail picks one independent interview (a media channel, not an institute) and reads its captions. A line counts as the topper's own statement only when it is in the first person, so a sponsor message read at the start of a video is never attributed to the topper. When the captions seem to contradict a claim, the page shows the line and its timestamp as words to check rather than a verdict.

Request a correction

If an institute discloses a course next to a claim and the ledger missed it, open an issue on the project repository with the page link. The page is collected again and the change is published with its date.
